Why Highland Cattle?

August 13, 2026

When we started looking at cattle for the place in Round Top, the usual options were on the table — commercial breeds, some of the popular heritage breeds, even a few of the smaller dual-purpose types. None of them felt quite right.

We wanted cattle. We also wanted animals we could actually manage as we get older. Full commercial programs require a different level of infrastructure and daily work than we were prepared to take on for a small, personal herd. We needed something that matched both the land and the season of life we’re in.

Highland cattle solved the problem. They keep the classic look — the long hair, the quiet expression, the sturdy Highland character — and they have a well-earned reputation for calm, curious, people-oriented temperaments when they’re raised with consistent, quiet handling.

A few practical advantages stood out for us:

Disposition. We are selecting hard for gentle, confident animals. The goal is cattle that are a pleasure to be around, not just productive.

Hardiness. Highlands were developed in a tough climate. They handle weather well, do fine on good forage, and generally stay healthy with basic care — including in Texas heat, where the coat actually helps with temperature regulation when managed properly.

Presence. They look like Highlands. That visual character is part of what we wanted on the property.

Scale that fits. A small fold — roughly ten cows — keeps feed, fencing, and handling realistic on modest acreage under a wildlife management plan. Quality over quantity.

We’re not trying to reinvent the breed or chase extremes. We want sound, correct, calm Highland cattle that can thrive in Texas and make good foundation animals for other small herds. If we include miniature Highlands in the fold, those animals will be registered with the International Miniature Cattle Breeders Registry (IMCBR). Our program is grounded in membership with the American Highland Cattle Association.

That’s the short version of why these cattle made sense for Round Top Fold. As the first animals arrive and we start learning the day-to-day reality, we’ll share more of what we discover.

— Drew
