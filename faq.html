Frequently Asked Questions

Straightforward answers about miniature Highland cattle, the Highland breed, and the town of Round Top, Texas.

Miniature Highland Cattle

What is a miniature Highland?

A miniature Highland is a smaller version of the traditional Scottish Highland breed, developed through selective breeding. Most true minis mature under about 42 inches at the hip (measured at three years of age). They keep the classic long hair, gentle expression, and hardy nature of standard Highlands, just in a more manageable size for small farms and families.

How big do mini Highlands get?

Mature miniature Highland cows typically stand in the mid-30s to low-40s inches at the hip and weigh roughly 500–800 pounds, depending on genetics and nutrition. Bulls run a bit larger. They are still real cattle — not “teacup” animals — but significantly easier to handle than full-size Highlands.

What is chondrodysplasia (chondro) and why does it matter?

Chondrodysplasia is a genetic condition that can produce very short-legged animals. When two carriers (chondro-positive) are bred together, there is a risk of producing a non-viable “bulldog” calf. Responsible breeders test for the gene and carefully plan pairings. At Round Top Fold we take this seriously and aim for sound, healthy structure in every animal we produce.

Are mini Highlands good pets or family animals?

Yes — when raised with consistent, calm handling. Highlands as a breed are known for a steady, curious, and docile temperament. Minis often become quite people-oriented. They still need proper fencing, pasture or hay, fresh water, and a companion (cattle are herd animals and should not live alone).

Do they have horns?

Traditional Highlands are horned. Many miniature programs include both horned and polled (naturally hornless) genetics. At Round Top Fold we are working toward polled cattle with gentle dispositions while preserving classic Highland character and hair.

How long do they live?

With good care, Highland cattle commonly live 15–20 years. They are a hardy, long-lived breed.

What do they eat and how much land do they need?

They are efficient grazers and do well on good pasture with supplemental hay when grass is limited. Free-choice minerals are important. Land needs vary with soil, rainfall, and management, but many small herds thrive on a few acres with proper rotational grazing and winter feed. They generally require less feed than larger commercial breeds.

How do mini Highlands handle Texas heat?

Surprisingly well. Scottish Highlands originated in a harsh climate and developed a double coat that actually helps regulate temperature — the long outer hair provides shade while the dense undercoat insulates. In Texas summers they shed heavily, seek shade, and do best with access to shade structures, trees, or a simple loafing area, plus plenty of clean water. Many Texas owners run them successfully with basic summer management.

Round Top, Texas

What is Round Top known for?

Round Top is a tiny historic town in Fayette County, roughly halfway between Houston and Austin. It is best known for the Round Top Antiques Shows — one of the largest and most influential antiques and design markets in the country. Three times a year the surrounding countryside fills with venues, barns, tents, and shoppers from across the U.S. and beyond.

How did the antiques shows begin?

The original show was founded in 1968 by Houston antiques dealer Emma Lee Turney. She was encouraged by local preservationists (including figures connected to the restoration of historic Texas homes) who wanted a high-quality country antiques fair in the area. The first event was held at the Round Top Rifle Hall with a small group of carefully chosen dealers. It has grown steadily ever since into the sprawling multi-venue phenomenon it is today.

When are the big shows?

The main shows typically take place in spring, fall, and a shorter winter session. Exact dates vary by venue. You can find current dates on our Local page and on the Explore Round Top events calendar. Lodging books up far in advance during show seasons.

Is Round Top only about antiques?

No. While the shows put the town on the map, Round Top and the surrounding countryside also offer a quieter year-round life of historic buildings, good food, music (Festival Hill), rolling farmland, and a strong sense of place. Many people fall in love with the area during a show and return for the slower pace the rest of the year.
